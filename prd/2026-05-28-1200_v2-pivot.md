# Pivot: v1 → v2 (Beyond Python's Backend)

**Date:** 2026-05-28
**Status:** Implemented (pivot complete, smoke test pending)
**Decision authority:** Project owner

## Goal

Pivot the project away from the v1 framing (*Rust Among Giants*, MICAI 2026, 10 languages including Python) to a sharper, more citable framing: *Beyond Python's Backend* — 9 languages compared as candidates for the performance-critical backend tier of AI/ML infrastructure systems.

## Background / Context

### Why pivot

Three forces converged to motivate this pivot, evaluated explicitly with the project owner on 2026-05-28:

1. **Python execution time was a major iteration bottleneck.** Python on B2 (bubble sort, N=10⁵) took ~2.4 hours per benchmark run (~11 min per iteration × 13 hyperfine runs). Across the 8-benchmark suite, Python represented an estimated 50–100% of total benchmark wall time. This severely slowed methodology iteration (e.g., adjusting workload sizes, re-running after bug fixes).

2. **Python's "is slow" finding is well-established and not novel.** Bugden 2022, Pereira 2017, and CLBG already document Python's 40–100× gap vs compiled languages on CPU-bound work. Re-measuring this in the paper consumes significant runtime without contributing a new result. The paper's most novel findings (Rust 10% behind C on tight loops due to bounds checks; JIT matches AOT on hot recursive code; Zig competitive with C) do not involve Python.

3. **AI/ML industrial reality is two-tier.** Real production AI systems use Python as orchestration over a compiled backend (PyTorch→C++/CUDA, HuggingFace→Rust, Polars→Rust, NumPy→C). The contested practitioner question is *which backend language wins*, not *is Python slow*. The pivoted paper directly addresses the question practitioners actually face.

### What stays the same

- The same 9 languages we already had (the 10 minus Python)
- The same 8 benchmarks (B1–B8)
- The same 5 metrics (M1–M5)
- The same hardware (Apple M1, macOS Sonoma)

### What changes

- **Title:** *Rust Among Giants* → *Beyond Python's Backend*
- **Subtitle:** *An Empirical Benchmark of Ten Programming Languages...* → *An Empirical Comparison of Nine Languages for High-Performance AI Infrastructure*
- **Target venue:** MICAI 2026 (deadline June 14, 2026) → top-tier PL/SE venues (PLDI 2027, OOPSLA 2026, ICSE 2027) or arXiv + technical blog for industry citation
- **Deadline:** No longer June 14, 2026 — allows quality over speed
- **Research questions:** Reframed around backend-tier choice (5 RQs in the new README)
- **Hypotheses:** 5 reformulated (H1–H5 in README) — preserve the original H1 (Rust ≈ C), refine H2 (JIT closes gap on hot code), add H3 (Zig viability), H4 (memory clustering), H5 (Julia trade-off)
- **Sections:** §1 Intro reframed; new §2 Scope and the Question of Python explicitly justifying exclusion; §6 Discussion structure pivoted to practitioner recommendations

## Risks and trade-offs accepted

- **Reviewer pushback on Python exclusion**: a reviewer at a generalist venue could ask "where is Python?" Our defense is the dedicated §2 (Scope and the Question of Python) plus the explicit acknowledgment that Python's gap is established by prior work. Acceptable risk.
- **Loss of direct Bugden 2022 comparison on Python**: Bugden included Python in their 6-language comparison. By dropping Python, our direct apples-to-apples extension is on the 5 non-Python languages they covered, plus our 4 additions. Acceptable; we discuss this in related work.
- **No fixed deadline**: removes June 14, 2026 pressure but also removes urgency. Mitigation: target OOPSLA 2026 (submission window typically March-April depending on round) or PLDI 2027 (submission typically late 2026).
